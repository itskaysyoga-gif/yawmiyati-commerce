Scope: short labels only. Competitor fees, times and return windows were left out on purpose.

## What this suggests for us (Saudi Arabia)
1. Saudi retail UI is **formal MSA almost everywhere**. Buttons, policies and help headings: "الدفع عند الاستلام", "الاستبدال والاسترجاع", "طلب الاسترداد", "اتصل بنا". *(formal / international evidence)*
2. Saudi dialect appears **only in short marketing taglines** (Carrefour: "لا تشيل همها حنًا نوصلها"). If we open SA, keep dialect to the hero strip or delivery reassurance lines.
3. Nahdi refers to the customer as "الضيف", a neutral and respectful convention that avoids gendered verbs. It is worth considering for policy text in all Gulf markets.
4. Useful order-status phrasing from Nahdi: "تأكيد الطلب وبدء تحضيره" for the confirmation step, and "الإبلاغ عن مشكلة" for the support entry point.
5. When COD is not available for something, Nahdi says so with a short negative label ("… غير متاح"). This pattern could serve our closed-market or out-of-area notice.
6. Sources default to masculine singular imperatives. No feminine address was observed.
7. Contrast only: BNPL and instalment wording is common in KSA (Home Centre). It does not apply to our COD-only journey.
8. Evidence is thin (2 web sources plus 1 app listing). Validate again before opening SA.
