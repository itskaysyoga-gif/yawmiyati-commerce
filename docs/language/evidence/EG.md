# Egypt (EG): Arabic interface wording evidence

Date inspected: 2026-10-29. Method: public pages read with WebFetch. WebFetch returns text pulled out of the page by a model, not the raw HTML. Phrases below are copied exactly as that tool returned them. Before shipping copy, check the exact characters on the live page.
Scope: short interface labels only. Competitor fees, delivery times, warranties and return windows were left out on purpose.

## 1. Rabbit (رابِت): Egyptian quick-commerce grocery. **Local Egyptian dialect source**
- URL: https://rabbitmart.com/ar/home-ar/ (Arabic marketing site). https://rabbitmart.com/ (English) also checked.
- Contexts: marketing headings, delivery wording, FAQ questions, CTA
- Examples:
  - "أي حاجة نفسك فيها، تلاقيها على بابك"
  - "نفسك في حاجة؟ جايالك بسرعة"
  - "ليه رابِت"
  - "قد إيه رابِت سريع؟" (FAQ)
  - "أي مدن بتغطّوها؟" (FAQ)
  - "أقدر أطلب إيه؟" (FAQ)
  - "في رسوم توصيل؟" (FAQ; we did not record their figures)
  - "حمّل الأبليكيشن"
  - "افتح الأبليكيشن، وسيبنا نجري إحنا"
  - "تواصل معنا" (nav label, MSA)
- Notes: FAQ questions are written in the customer's own voice (أقدر أطلب إيه؟). Company and careers sections switch to MSA.
- Address: masculine or unmarked singular (افتح، نفسك). Many forms such as تلاقيها and بيتك are gender-neutral in writing.

## What this suggests for us (Egypt)
1. **Use Egyptian dialect for marketing lines, delivery promises and FAQ questions.** The local quick-commerce brands write this way (Rabbit, Breadfast): "تلاقيها على بابك", "لحد باب البيت", "أقدر أطلب إيه؟". *(local dialect evidence)*
2. **Use MSA for buttons, navigation and policy headings.** Every store checked does, local and international: "الدفع عند الاستلام", "الاسترجاع و التبديل", "اتصل بنا", "تواصل معنا". Rabbit keeps "تواصل معنا" in MSA even inside dialect copy.
3. Recurring natural terms: "الدفع عند الاستلام" (COD), "توصيل" (delivery), "الاسترجاع والاستبدال/التبديل" (returns), "خدمة العملاء" (support; Egypt uses العملاء, not الزبائن), "طلباتي", "تتبّع طلبك".
4. **Write FAQ questions in first person, from the customer's side.** Rabbit does it in dialect (أقدر أطلب إيه؟), B.TECH and Talabat in MSA (هل يمكنني…). Dialect fits our audience better.
5. Egyptian dialect markers that read naturally in short UI-adjacent copy: إيه، ليه، أقدر، اللي، لحد، أكتر، بتوصلك، تلاقي، إحنا. Keep them to explanatory and marketing text.
6. **No Egyptian source used feminine address.** Masculine or unmarked forms are the convention. For a women-focused store, prefer gender-neutral constructions: verbal nouns ("إتمام الطلب"), first-person questions, and suffixes like -ك that do not show gender. Feminine imperatives (اطلبي) would be a deliberate brand choice, not a local norm.
7. Avoid stiff MSA future and passive forms in customer-facing explanations (سيتم رد ثمن…). Egyptian readers see "هيتم" or a plain active verb in friendly copy, but policies can stay in simple MSA.
8. Avoid English loanwords like "الأبليكيشن" unless we have an app. Rabbit uses it, but for a web store "الموقع" is clearer.
9. Contrast only: Carrefour has "الدفع بأمان عبر الانترنت" and several sources mention installments and cards. We are COD-only, so do not adopt this wording.
